Where your data goes

Every step runs inside your Mac.

Most AI note takers send your audio to a server to be transcribed and summarised. Juanola does both on your Mac's GPU, so the meeting never has to go anywhere.

Don't take our word for it

Privacy you can check for yourself.

“We don't look at your data” is a promise. With Juanola there's nothing to promise, because your data never reaches anyone. You can confirm it in five minutes.

  1. 1

    Turn the Wi‑Fi off

    Once the models are downloaded, record a meeting in airplane mode. You still get the transcript and the summary.

  2. 2

    Watch the network

    Run Little Snitch, LuLu or nettop while you record. Juanola makes no connections.

  3. 3

    Read the code

    It's MIT-licensed. Only one module can reach the internet, crates/models, and all it does is download the models.

  4. 4

    A locked-down interface

    The app's content security policy blocks the interface from loading or sending anything outside your Mac.

  5. 5

    Delete everything

    Your transcripts and notes are a single SQLite file. Delete it, or delete a note and empty the trash, and no copy is left anywhere else.

How it works

Private doesn't mean worse notes.

1

It listens on two channels

Your microphone and your Mac's system audio are captured separately, so “Me” and “Them” never get mixed up. It works with Zoom, Meet, Teams and Slack, with no bot, invite or plugin.

2

It transcribes as you talk

Draft text appears while people are still talking, and each sentence is finalised a few seconds after it ends. The meeting's title, attendees and glossary come from your calendar on this Mac and help get names right.

3

It enhances your notes

When the call ends, a local model merges what you wrote with what was said. Your words stay black and its additions are grey. The ⌕ on every line opens the exact moment in the transcript.

The models on your Mac

Small models, measured on real work.

Open-weight models, about 3.5 GB in total (2.6 GB on Macs with less than 16 GB of RAM). They’re downloaded once from inside the app and checked against their SHA-256, and after that they run with no connection. Every change to the pipeline is measured against scripted meetings and logged, kept or thrown out.
